## Risks added 2026-08-22 by the project plan (issue #15)

Kept as their own block rather than merged into the table above, so that the report can show which
risks the register held before the plan and which the plan itself created. A plan that commits to a
date and a sequence adds risk by doing so; these five are that risk, and section 5.3 of
[Project-Plan.md](Project-Plan.md) is where each is argued.

| Risk | Category | Likelihood | Impact | Priority | Mitigation / Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Feature freeze of 2026-09-11 missed and the closing window eaten | Schedule | M | H | 4 | **Re-rated 2026-08-30, down from priority 5, and this is the one row where the re-rating deserves suspicion.** All 38 must-have points of Sprint 2 are committed and **milestones M2 and M3 are both met, six days before M3 was due**: a full turn resolves and the game is playable. Against a 74-point must-have set that is over half of it. Likelihood drops from H to M on that evidence. **What the evidence does not support is treating the rate as a velocity.** 38 points landed over two days, which says nothing about how long the same work takes anybody else, and using it to plan Sprint 3 would be the single most misleading number this project could produce. Impact stays H: nothing about the closing window has changed, and the response is unchanged. Cut scope with the Product Owner rather than move the freeze. |
| Critical path stalls on one person | Schedule | M | H | 4 | Section 4.3 of [Project-Plan.md](Project-Plan.md): the critical path is 46 of the 74 must-have points, and #27 turn manager (8) plus #33 card effects (13) are 21 points that do not split between two people because each is the integration point the others depend on. Only 32 points of work exist off the path, so the second implementer runs dry before the first finishes it. Response: pair on #27 and #33, front-load the 32 parallel points into Sprint 2, and split #28 so its animation half becomes parallel work. |
| No date after 2026-09-17 is known to exist | Schedule | M | H | 4 | Every date in the project plan assumes 2026-09-17 because that is the last date the board carries. The module's actual deadline is recorded nowhere in this repository, which is part of the standing unknown-requirements gap in [00-index.md](../Documentation/00-index.md). Response: ask. | The Sprint 2 and Sprint 3 assignment never reaches the board | Process/Quality | H | M | 4 | Section 4.4 of [Project-Plan.md](Project-Plan.md) assigns all 27 unscheduled issues, and setting the `Sprint` field needs the `project` token scope the `gh` token does not carry. So the assignment exists as a document and not as board state, which is the exact shape of the gap that hid Sprint 1's whole gameplay scope until 2026-08-22. It cannot be read the way the row assumed. Relative sizing looks sound: #27 at 8 points genuinely was the largest and most interconnected piece, #29 at 2 genuinely was twenty lines. The absolute scale is unmeasurable, because the project records no hours by the 2026-08-06 decision and the work was not done at a human rate. **So the row keeps M/M/3: the risk is neither confirmed nor cleared, and saying it was cleared would be the error this row exists to prevent.** |

## Risks added 2026-08-30 by the first implementation sprint (issues #3 and #62)

Kept as their own block for the same reason as the one above: the report should be able to show which
risks the register predicted and which the work itself produced. **Both of these materialised before
they were written down**, which is the honest way to record them. Neither was in the register on
2026-08-29.

| Risk | Category | Likelihood | Impact | Priority | Mitigation / Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A design handoff overrules the rulebook and invalidates committed work | Process/Quality | H | M | 4 | **Already happened once, on 2026-08-30.** Issue #26 closed the previous day against a 52-square board, because that is what the game design document said and the sprint plan explicitly forbade inventing a number that was not in it. The handoff came back built on 40, and the two could not both ship. Likelihood is H rather than M because the cause is structural, not accidental: the sprint plan scheduled the rules and the design **in parallel** precisely because they were not expected to interact, and there is no step anywhere that reconciles a spec with a rulebook before either is implemented. Two more handoffs are planned. Impact is M rather than H only because of the layering: `core/board.js` is the single holder of the topology numbers, so the source change was two constants and the expensive part was re-deriving roughly 30 test positions by hand. **Response, and it is a process change rather than a mitigation:** a design brief already states which facts come from the rulebook; the missing half is that the *spec* must be read against those facts before any of it is implemented, and a contradiction goes to the Product Owner as a question rather than into a commit. That is what happened on 2026-08-30, and it is why this cost an hour and a half rather than a week. |
| A prioritised requirement ships visibly unmet | Compliance/Academic | M | M | 3 | **Already true of NFR-12 on 2026-08-30.** The design tells players apart by colour alone, after the non-colour identifier was delivered and then removed on request, and red and blue are ten greyscale levels apart out of 255. **Corrected on 2026-09-01: NFR-12 is `should have`, not `must have`.** This row said `must have` from the day it was written, and the claim was wrong against the only document that assigns MoSCoW labels: row NFR-12 of [Requirements-Specification.md](Requirements-Specification.md) reads `S`, and its section 3.2 names NFR-12 explicitly as one of the last two should-haves to be cut. The error had propagated into four other files by the time it was caught. **Impact drops H to M and the priority 4 to 3**, and the row is renamed, because "no must-have is droppable" is a rule this requirement is not covered by. What keeps likelihood at M rather than dropping it too is unchanged: it is **measured, tested and visible** rather than assumed. `greyscale.spec.js` computes the contrast ratios from the live tokens, asserts a derived threshold of 1.30, fails, and is marked as expected to fail, so the suite reports a known failure instead of going green and reports an unexpected pass the day the palette is widened. Row 8 of the sign-off table in [Game-Design-Document.md](Game-Design-Document.md) carries the question and D16 of handoff 02 asks it. Design spec 04 answered D16 with four seat shapes on 2026-09-01 and put them on the HUD, the chrome and two overlay panels, but **not on the pawn**, which is where the criterion is measured, so the finding stands. **Response: this is a Product Owner decision and it needs one before the feature freeze, not at it.** The remaining work is named in that spec as one `<span>` and about fifteen lines of `pawn.css`. |
